Sessions

The list shows every Claude Code session on the computer, whichever app started it: the terminal, Claude Desktop, VS Code or Pelican. A dot marks what changed since you last looked, and a bar marks the session that is open. A session’s title is the name you gave it or the one Claude Code wrote; without one, the text of a prompt, cut to one line, never one of Claude Desktop’s requests from Changes (Commit changes, Create PR). The browser tab shows the open session and how many sessions need you.

Search covers titles, folders and the latest reply. Filter by project; sessions started without a project share one Scratch filter, and runs in the system’s temp folder (usually started by another session) stay out of All unless the list’s options say otherwise (where they are all there is, the list says how many there are, with their Temporary chip and Show them); a folder there that you started a session in from Pelican (a checkout in /tmp, picked with Browse folders) is a project like any other, its sessions listed and the folder offered again under New session. Group by time or by project, with detailed or compact rows, from the button next to the search. Long lists load as you scroll, and a row opens in a new tab with ⌘-click (Ctrl-click).

A session whose turn is over but whose background work still runs shows how much, as 2 in background with a steady dot, among the active ones; it says Working again when Claude takes up what they found. A session Claude Code stopped before it could reply says why instead of a reply, such as Stopped: not signed in.

To keep a session at the top of the list, pin it: on a phone, swipe its row a little to the left and tap Pin; on a computer, pick Pin in the row’s ⋯, which shows in place of its time when you point at the row; or use Pin in the session menu. Pinned sessions lead the list under Pinned, however it is grouped, and under their project’s filter; each keeps its status and its earlier runs. To archive a session, swipe its row all the way to the left, tap Archive beside Pin, or pick Archive in its menu. A row takes no swipe to the right, which in Safari is Back, nor one that starts at the very edge of the screen, which the browser keeps for itself, nor one while text is selected. Archived sessions wait under the Archived filter, to be unarchived the same way. Undo shows for five seconds after either, whichever way you did it. Archiving a session takes it off the list and unpins it; nothing else changes. Pins and the archive are Pelican’s own, the same on your phone and your computer, and apart from Claude Desktop’s: a session pinned in Desktop is not pinned here.

+ opens an empty session in your latest project, or the project the list shows, or a new folder under Scratch, ready to type. Tap the folder under What should Claude do? to start somewhere else.

Sessions another app is running

Pelican sees every session Claude Code runs on the computer. One that another app is working on says Working in Claude Desktop (or the terminal, or VS Code), in the list and in its header, and you can follow it as it works. One that waits for you there, at a permission prompt or a question, says Needs you in the terminal, and if the wait lasts about 20 seconds your phone gets one notification; answer at the keyboard first and nothing is sent. Pelican cannot answer that prompt for you: answer it in that app, or with Remote Control.

The message box says what sending would do, for example Working in the terminal · a message starts a second Claude. A session another app has open but idle says so at the foot of the session menu. Continue a session in one place at a time: before it sends, Pelican catches up with what was said in the other app, but the other app does not see what you send here until the session is reopened there. Side chats, Claude Desktop’s and Pelican’s, are kept out of the list; Side chats in the list’s options shows them.

Where every action is

Each thing you act on has one short list of actions, in Claude Desktop’s words and order, and every way to it shows the same list: a session, your prompt, Claude’s reply, a step, and a file’s path. On a computer, right-click the thing, or use its ⋯ where it has one; Desktop’s key for an action shows faintly beside it. On a phone, press and hold the thing for half a second: a session’s row, your prompt, a step or a path rises over a sheet of its actions. A long press on Claude’s words stays your phone’s own, for selecting text.

  • The session menu is the session’s title, with ▾: Rename, Pin, Side chat, Fork (a full copy), Copy resume command (to carry on in a terminal), Export (as Markdown), Transcript view, Archive and Delete, which asks first and waits while Claude works. Its foot says when the session started, in which app it is open as well, if one has it, and its ID, to copy. A row of the list has the same menu from its ⋯, a right-click or a long press, without the view and the keys, which are the open session’s. On a phone the menu starts with Changes, Files and Terminal.
  • The header has, on a computer, three quiet buttons: Changes, with the lines changed (+31 −2), Files and Terminal, each with its name and key when you point at it. A phone has one button, the lines changed, or a folder while nothing has.
  • Your prompt: on a phone, a tap shows Copy, Rewind to here and Fork from here under it, and a long press has Quote and Select text as well; on a computer, Copy, Rewind to here and Fork from here show beside it when you point at it, and a right-click has the rest. A left swipe quotes it.
  • Claude’s reply has Copy, ⋯ and its time under it, always on a phone, and on a computer when you point at it: Copy, Fork from here, ⋯, the model and the time. Its ⋯ has Copy message, Copy message as Markdown, Start a side chat, Quote message, Rewind to here and Fork from here, and the turn’s model, time, tokens and cost at its foot. A right-click puts what you clicked first: Copy and Quote selection for text you selected, Copy code on a code block, a path’s own menu on a path; a link or a picture keeps the browser’s own menu.
  • A step: a command has Copy command, Run in terminal, Copy output, Start a side chat and Quote output; an agent View output; a step on a file has its path’s menu. On a computer it has a ⋯ when you point at it. A ! command has a command’s list too. Run in terminal asks first, in place, as a code block’s does, showing the whole command rather than the step’s description of it, and is offered for a command that shows all it holds, as a code block’s is; not on a step you denied, nor on a subagent’s or a workflow’s steps, and on a PowerShell step only where the terminal runs PowerShell.
  • A path, in a reply, a step, a row of Changes or Files, or under a file’s ⋯, opens its file with a tap or a click, and its menu is Desktop’s: Attach as context (the file goes with your next message, as @"path"), Copy its absolute or relative path or its name, Open in Changes for a file with changes, Open in Files and Download file; a folder has Open in terminal.
  • The message box’s + has Add files or photos, Commands, Files and agents, Shell command and the session’s MCP servers.
  • Context, the ring by Send, shows how full the context window is, with Compact, what the whole session used, your plan’s limits, and Usage and activity. On a phone it shows once a turn has run, faint and without its number below 60%.
  • One list of commands sits behind / in the message box and, on a computer, ⌘K (Ctrl K): this session’s actions by Claude Code’s names (/btw, /branch, /rewind, /rename, /export, /diff, /context, /mcp, /model, /usage), then Pelican’s (Pin, Archive, Files, Terminal, New session, Settings), then Claude Code’s commands and skills. One of your own commands with the name of one of Pelican’s is yours: /export then runs it, and Pelican’s Export stays in the list by its name. In ⌘K’s list, typing a session’s name finds it too.

On a computer the session list hides with its button at its top, or ⌘B (Ctrl B), as in Claude Desktop, and the browser remembers it; the conversation then takes the whole width, with the button first in its header. Point at the window’s left edge, or at that button, and the list comes over the conversation until the pointer leaves it, you pick a session, or Esc; ⇧⌘K brings it to search. Between 1024 and 1279 pixels wide, a pane beside the conversation hides the list the same way, and ⌘B or the button floats it over the conversation, which keeps its room, until ⌘B again, Esc, a click outside it or a pick. On a phone, Back (Android’s, or the browser’s) closes the sheet or menu that is open, and goes no further.

Reading a session

Three views, as in Claude Desktop: Normal folds the steps and shows the replies whole, Thinking adds Claude’s thinking, and Verbose opens every step. Each session keeps the one picked in its menu; the others open in the one picked in Settings, and Ctrl O steps through them. Tool calls show what they did the way you would check it: diffs for edits, the command and its output, images. Plans and questions get their own cards, with what you answered under them. Claude’s to-do list is one line, Tasks · 1 of 4 done, which opens to the list as it was then; while there is work left, the bar above the message box shows where it stands, the task under way first; an Agent call opens the steps its subagent took, and a workflow is a card with its phases and agents (below).

A file a step names opens at its line, where a read began or an edit changed it, and so does a path:line in a reply (also :7-8 and #L3-L9), when the file is in the session’s folder. Under a turn that edited files, 3 files changed opens what that turn changed.

A session opens at its newest message, and while Claude works the view follows what it writes. Scroll up and it stays where you are, with a round arrow to jump back to the end. The whole history is there, up to the first prompt; a divider marks each compaction, and opens to the summary Claude carried on from. Long prompts fold after about twelve lines, and files you sent show as chips that open them. Code shows with its language and a Copy button, highlighted in the common languages.

For a session running in Pelican, the last reply’s ⋯ has that turn’s time, tokens and cost, and a ring next to Send shows how full the context is. The cost is left out for Claude plans, whose usage limits Context and Usage and activity show instead, and for the first turn after a session is resumed. When a request fails, one line says why in plain words: the API is overloaded, the conversation is too long, Claude Code is not signed in (run claude and /login), or the usage limit was reached, and when it resets. At the end of the conversation it offers the next step: Retry sends the prompt again with its files, and Compact sends /compact when the conversation is too long.
